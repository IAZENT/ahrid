# Mock Written Examination — ST6047CEM (2–3 Hour Essay Format)

Related: [[00 - Exam Prep Hub]] | [[08 - Full Essay Model Answers]]

> [!danger] Attempt this cold, under a real timer (choose 2 or 2.5 hours depending on which your actual exam turns out to be), before reading [[08 - Full Essay Model Answers]]. Write full essay-length answers — aim for the same depth and structure as the model answers, roughly 400–600 words per major question, ~2000 words total.

**Time allowed: 2–3 hours | Target total output: ~2000 words**

---

## Section A — Foundational Context (20 marks, ~20 min, ~250 words)
*One combined short-essay answer.*

**A1.** In a single connected response (not bullet points), state your two research questions, your two hypotheses, and whether each hypothesis was confirmed — with the specific evidence for each.

---

## Section B — Extended Justification Essays (50 marks, ~90 min, ~1000 words)
*Choose to answer in full-essay form. Examiners are testing sustained, structured argument — claim, development, honest limitation, tie-back to RQ.*

**B1.** (25 marks, ~500 words) Justify the design of your three-stage machine learning pipeline (K-Means → Random Forest → SHAP) and explain precisely how it answers RQ1. Include the specific alternatives you considered and rejected at each stage, and be explicit about what your results do and do not prove.

**B2.** (25 marks, ~500 words) Trace the complete lifecycle of a single employee's data through AHRID, from initial consent to eventual deletion. At each stage, name the specific technical mechanism and connect it to the relevant IPA 2075 principle it satisfies.

---

## Section C — Ethical Defence Essay (30 marks, ~40 min, ~600 words)
*This section carries disproportionate weight in how it will be judged — treat it as the section to spend the most careful thought on, even if not the most raw time.*

**C1.** An examiner states: *"Regardless of your stated intentions, AHRID is still a workplace surveillance system — you collect continuous behavioural data on employees and use it to classify them. How is this meaningfully different from a system designed to punish low performers, and what specifically in your architecture, not your intentions, supports your answer?"*

Write a full response that: (a) concedes the legitimate part of the concern, (b) names the specific architectural mechanisms (RBAC scope, absence of a disciplinary data path, consent/withdrawal support, SHAP transparency) that distinguish AHRID from punitive surveillance, and (c) honestly states the limit of this defence — what technical controls cannot guarantee.

## What "thorough" looks like at this length — self-check
- [ ] Did every major essay answer include **at least one honest limitation**, not just a defence?
- [ ] Did you name **specific alternatives you rejected** (not just the choice you made)?
- [ ] Did every essay explicitly **tie back to RQ1 or RQ2** in its final sentence?
- [ ] Did your Section C answer **concede something real** before defending, rather than opening with pure defence?
- [ ] Did you hit roughly the target word count per section, or did one section eat all your time? (Practice pacing — a stopwatch per question helps.)

## Timing calibration
| If your exam is... | Section A | Section B | Section C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2 hours** | 15 min | 75 min | 30 min |
| **2.5 hours** | 20 min | 95 min | 35 min |
| **3 hours** | 25 min | 115 min | 40 min |

Always leave the last 5 minutes of any exam to reread your Section C answer once — it's the section most likely to be judged on how carefully you conceded the examiner's point before defending.

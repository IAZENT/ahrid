================================================================================
AHRIP - DESIGN THINKING DELIVERABLES
Adaptive Human Risk Intelligence Platform
Context: Kathmandu-based employees (Himalayan Finance Ltd)
================================================================================

Product: AHRIP is a cybersecurity awareness training webapp that uses a
mastery-based adaptive engine, Random Forest risk scoring, K-Means user
clustering, SHAP explainability, and OSINT threat feeds (Phishing.Database,
AlienVault OTX) with rule-based scenario generation.
Targets employees at Kathmandu Valley organizations.

What USER (Employee) sees when opening the webapp:
- Dashboard: Risk score, sessions completed, weekly trend
- Training: Adaptive MCQ session (20 questions), timer, category badge, difficulty
- My Score: Detailed risk breakdown by category
- History: Past session results
- Profile: Personal details, job role, department
- Evaluation: Self-assessment form

What ADMIN sees when opening the webapp:
- Admin Dashboard: Total users, active scenarios, threats ingested (24h), attempts (24h), ML model status, background job controls (Ingest Feed / Retrain Models)
- Users: Create, edit, deactivate, assign roles/job_roles
- Scenarios: CRUD for training items (MCQ, T/F, identify_threat)
- Threat Feed: Ingested phishing URLs, brand targets, conversion status
- Password Resets: Approve/reject employee reset requests
- Evaluation Admin: View evaluation responses

What MANAGER sees:
- Team Intelligence: Avg risk, high/critical count, sessions/week, trend
- Team: Individual member profiles, assign targeted training
- Clusters: K-Means archetype distribution
- Reports: Aggregated analytics

1. EMPATHY MAP
================================================================================
Persona: Suman Shrestha, 28, Accountant at Himalayan Finance Ltd, Putalisadak
Uses Tally, Excel, NIC Asia Bank portal daily. Commutes from Lalitpur.

SEES:
- AHRIP dashboard showing risk score number and a colored risk-level badge
- Scenario cards with phishing email content (fake NIC Asia login pages)
- Progress bar showing question 4/20 during a training session
- Session summary at the end: accuracy ring, duration, weakest category
- Colleagues in the open-plan office completing training at their desks
- Timer counting seconds while reading a scenario question

SAYS:
- "I finished my training for this week, done."
- "This phishing email looks like the real NIC Asia one, how am I supposed to tell?"
- "My risk score dropped from 72 to 65 but I don't feel any safer."
- "Can I do this later? Month-end reconciliation is due today."

THINKS:
- "My risk score is a number but I don't know what it actually measures."
- "The card says 'Selected for your accounting role' but I still don't get it."
- "If I answer fast, I get easier questions next time. System rewards speed."
- "My risk score dropped but I still clicked a suspicious link last week."
- "I see accuracy % at the end but forget it by the next session."

DOES:
- Rushes through 20-question sessions during lunch break to finish fast
- Selects answers quickly without reading full scenario content
- Glances at session summary accuracy % then closes the page immediately
- Skips reading the feedback explanation after wrong answers
- Opens training during month-end then gets interrupted by Tally alerts

FEELS:
- Frustrated that training interrupts actual accounting deadlines
- Anxious about the visible risk score - colleagues or manager might judge
- Bored despite the role-relevance label - the scenarios still feel like a test
- Indifferent toward the mastery percentage shown in feedback - just a number
- Confused by the My Score page (risk score, RF prediction, cluster archetype)

KEY INSIGHT (Employee):
Suman completes sessions to get them done, not to learn. The system shows him
a risk score and mastery % but these numbers don't change how he thinks about
security. The gap between "finishing 20 questions" and "understanding why that
fake NIC Asia login was dangerous" is where real vulnerability lives.

Works remotely from Bhaktapur. Manages AHRIP platform, monitors threat feeds.

SEES:
- Admin dashboard with total users, active scenarios, threats ingested (24h)
- ML model status panel (Random Forest trained/untrained, last retrained date)
- Background job controls: "Ingest Feed" and "Retrain Models" buttons
- User table with names, roles, risk scores, cluster labels, active status
- Threat feed list showing ingested phishing URLs, target brands, conversion %
- Password reset requests queue waiting for approval

SAYS:
- "The risk score says 72 but I don't know which category is causing it."
- "I retrained the model but nothing visibly changed for the employees."
- "Three users are flagged high-risk but I have no recommended action."
- "The feed ingested 15 new threats but only 2 got converted to scenarios."
- "Management wants a report but I can only export what the dashboard shows."

THINKS:
- "I can see numbers but I can't explain them to my manager in simple terms."
- "Why doesn't the system suggest what to do with high-risk users?"
- "Retraining the model feels like a black box - did it actually improve?"
- "I wish I could assign targeted training without manually checking each user."
- "The cluster labels (moderate_risk, low_engagement) mean nothing to non-IT staff."

DOES:
- Checks admin dashboard every morning, looks at threats ingested count
- Manually clicks "Retrain Models" once a week without knowing if it helped
- Scrolls through user list looking for high-risk scores by eye
- Approves password reset requests one by one
- Creates new scenarios manually when threat feed conversion misses local context
- Exports data by copy-pasting from the browser for monthly reports

FEELS:
- Overwhelmed by data without actionable recommendations
- Frustrated that the ML pipeline is a black box with no feedback
- Responsible for org-wide security but lacks tools to intervene precisely
- Isolated - manages the system alone, no second admin to verify decisions
- Uncertain whether retraining or ingesting actually improves employee outcomes

KEY INSIGHT (Admin):
Rajesh has access to powerful ML outputs (RF scores, clusters, SHAP) but lacks
actionable interpretation. The admin dashboard shows WHAT is happening but not
WHY or WHAT TO DO NEXT. He needs the system to recommend interventions, not
just display metrics. The gap is between "data visibility" and "decision support."

2. FIVE WHYS
================================================================================

PROBLEM: Employees at Himalayan Finance treat AHRIP training as a pointless
task - they either don't see why it's their responsibility, find it boring
and irrelevant to their actual job, rush through it without thinking, and
ultimately remain just as vulnerable to real attacks as before.
(Links to: Empathy Map - Suman's Thinks/Feels; POEMS - friction points;
POV Statement - the core user need.)

1. Why do employees not take cybersecurity training seriously?
   -> Because they believe cybersecurity is the IT department's job. Suman is
   an accountant - he thinks hackers attack servers and firewalls, not people
   who process invoices. He sees the training as something IT made him do,
   not something that protects him personally.
   (Links to: Empathy Map - Suman Says: "Can I do this later?"; Admin Empathy
   Map - Rajesh Feels: responsible but lacks tools to convince employees.)

2. Why do they believe they are not personally at risk?
   -> Because the training content does not connect to their daily workflow
   in a tangible way. The system uses role-category priorities and shows
   a transparency label ("Selected for your accounting role: phishing email
   detection"), but the scenarios themselves remain abstract MCQ questions.
   Suman never sees a scenario that looks like his actual NIC Asia Bank
   portal or his Tally interface - so despite the label, he still feels
   the training is hypothetical and not about him personally.
   (Links to: Empathy Map - Suman Thinks: "Label says accounting but the
   question still feels generic"; POEMS - Environment: competing Tally.)

3. Why doesn't abstract MCQ training feel personally relevant?
   -> Because reading about phishing in a multiple-choice format is
   fundamentally different from encountering a convincing fake email in
   one's inbox. The adaptive engine correctly targets Suman's weak
   categories and the card explains why, but the training modality
   (read → pick A-D → see feedback) lacks the visceral impact of a
   real attack simulation. The employee learns facts but doesn't build
   the reflexive suspicion needed when a real threat arrives.
   (Links to: POEMS - Friction: training modality gap;
   Empathy Map - Suman Feels: bored despite role-relevance label.)

4. Why do employees rush through it anyway instead of engaging?
   -> Because even when they do sit down to train, there is no consequence
   for rushing. The system accepts an answer whether Suman spent 2 seconds
   or 2 minutes on a question. The training ends the moment he submits -
   no follow-up, no reflection, no revisiting mistakes. The mastery score
   updates silently in the background. So the fastest way to "complete"
   training is to click through and move on. The scenarios feel irrelevant,
   and rushing has no downside - so that's exactly what employees do.
   (Links to: Empathy Map - Suman Does: rushes through sessions; Feels:
   bored by generic scenarios. POEMS - Friction: fast answers reduce
   difficulty, making future sessions even easier.)

5. Why does the system reward finishing instead of understanding?
   -> Because the organisation needs proof of compliance - "all staff
   completed training this quarter." The platform was built to generate
   trackable numbers (risk scores, mastery %, completion counts) that
   satisfy audit requirements and look good on the admin dashboard. It was
   not designed to change how employees actually behave when a real phishing
   email lands in their inbox tomorrow morning.
   (Links to: Admin Empathy Map - Rajesh Thinks: "I see numbers but can't
   explain them"; Rajesh Feels: uncertain whether retraining improves
   outcomes. POV Statement - the gap between compliance and real security.)

ROOT CAUSE:
The platform was built to measure compliance ("did they finish?") not to
change behaviour ("do they understand?"). Employees don't engage because:
- They don't believe security is their problem (no personal relevance shown)
- The content is role-matched with transparency labels but still feels abstract
- Rushing is rewarded the same as careful learning (no comprehension check)
- There is no follow-up or reinforcement after training ends
All four issues stem from the same root: the system optimises for numbers
that satisfy audits, not for experiences that change how people think.

3. POEMS OBSERVATION
================================================================================

PEOPLE:
- Suman Shrestha (accountant) completing training at his desk during lunch
- Anita Maharjan (receptionist) answering phones while doing training session
- Rajesh Tamang (IT admin) monitoring threat dashboard remotely from Bhaktapur
- Deepa Adhikari (HR) reviewing team compliance reports in meeting room
- Manager checking cluster distribution and top-risk users periodically

OBJECTS:
- Admin dashboard: RF model status, cluster summary, ingestion/retrain buttons
- Scenario cards: MCQ with 4 options, category badge, difficulty indicator
- Employee dashboard: Risk score card, sessions completed, weekly trend
- Training interface: Progress bar, timer, answer options (A-D), feedback card
- My Score page: SHAP explanation panel, RF prediction, cluster archetype
- Session summary: Accuracy ring, duration, categories covered, learning tips

ENVIRONMENT:
- Open-plan office at Putalisadak, Kathmandu - frequent interruptions
- Remote home setup (staff from Lalitpur, Bhaktapur) with family distractions
- Slow internet during load-shedding hours affecting page loads
- Mobile use during Kathmandu commute (microbus/tempo)
- Competing attention: Tally, Excel, NIC Asia portal, phone calls
- Peak banking hours (9-11 AM, month-end) create cognitive overload

MESSAGES:
- "Session complete: 14/20 correct · 180s · 4 categories covered"
- "Focus area: Your weakest category was phishing_email"
- "Risk level raised to high - review your weak categories"
- "New training assignment from your manager: phishing_email, data_handling"
- Feedback card per question: correct answer, explanation, red flags, learning tip

SERVICES:
- Mastery-based adaptive scenario selection engine (role-category priorities)
- OSINT threat feed pipeline (Phishing.Database, OTX)
- Random Forest risk classifier (14-feature vector, behavioural proxies)
- K-Means user clustering (5 archetypes, 6 features)
- SHAP explainability for per-user risk factor explanation
- Rule-based scenario generator (6 lure templates, no LLM)
- Spaced repetition (correctly answered scenarios spaced 12-72h by risk level)
- Notification service (risk_escalation, training_assigned)

FRICTION POINTS OBSERVED:
- Suman answers fast -> difficulty drops -> easier questions -> false low risk
  (Links to: 5 Whys - Why 4: no consequence for rushing)
- Timer advances during interruptions (phone rings, colleague asks question)
- Transparency label shows role-relevance but training modality remains abstract
  (Links to: 5 Whys - Why 3: label present but MCQ format limits impact)
- New hires confused by multiple metrics (risk score, mastery, RF prediction,
  cluster archetype) with no guidance on what they mean
  (Links to: Empathy Map - Suman Feels: confused by My Score page)
- Month-end reconciliation period: zero training engagement for 3-5 days
  (Links to: Empathy Map - Suman Says: "Can I do this later?")
- Admin sees raw risk numbers but wants actionable coaching recommendations
  (Links to: Empathy Map - Rajesh Thinks: "I see numbers but can't act")

OPPORTUNITY:
- Add micro-reflection prompts after wrong answers
- Pause timer during detected inactivity
- Reward reasoning quality, not just answer correctness
- Schedule notifications outside peak reconciliation hours (avoid 9-11 AM)
- Introduce interactive phishing simulations alongside MCQ-based training

================================================================================
SECTION 3: FEASIBLE SOLUTIONS (10 per problem)
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P1: Employees rush through training without reading content
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  Add minimum read-time per scenario (e.g., 15s before submit enables)
2.  Show a brief comprehension check after the explanation card
3.  Factor time-spent-reading into mastery/risk calculation, not just correctness
4.  Introduce reflection prompt after wrong answers requiring typed response
5.  Reduce session length from 20 to 10 questions but add depth per question
6.  Display scenario relevance tag ("Why this matters for your role")
7.  Require viewing the full explanation before advancing to next question
8.  Flag sessions completed under 3 minutes as "low engagement" in admin view
9.  Show engagement quality score alongside risk score to differentiate depth
10. Tighten spaced repetition for missed scenarios (shorter re-appearance window)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P2: Admin cannot interpret or act on risk scores meaningfully
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  Add per-category risk breakdown visible on admin user detail page
2.  Show SHAP feature importance for each user's RF prediction
3.  Generate automated coaching recommendations per high-risk user
4.  Add "weakest category" column to the admin users table
5.  Create one-click "assign remedial training" button from admin dashboard
6.  Show historical risk trend (last 4 weeks) for each user on hover
7.  Add notification to admin when a user's score crosses critical threshold
8.  Display cluster archetype description (not just label) on dashboard
9.  Export risk report per department as CSV for management review
10. Add natural-language summary: "User X struggles with phishing_email
    due to 3 consecutive wrong answers in last 2 sessions"

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P3: Training feedback exists but employees do not use it to improve
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  Require reading the full explanation before the "Next" button appears
2.  Add a 1-question micro-quiz after the explanation to confirm understanding
3.  Highlight the mastery % change prominently in the feedback card itself
4.  Show "Your weakest area this session" summary before allowing session end
5.  Send a notification after each session: "You struggled with phishing_email"
6.  Make the session summary page the default landing after training
7.  Add a "Review mistakes" button on the session summary to revisit wrong answers
8.  Show per-category mastery trend on the employee dashboard (not just My Score)
9.  Use the SHAP explanation in employee-facing language on the dashboard
10. Prompt employee with "Did you know?" micro-facts related to their wrong answers

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P4: Training engagement drops to zero during peak work periods
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  Auto-detect low-engagement periods and send lighter "welcome back" session
2.  Allow employees to set "busy hours" where notifications are suppressed
3.  Offer 5-question micro-sessions during busy periods (instead of 20)
4.  Schedule training reminders based on individual activity patterns (avoid 9-11 AM)
5.  Send a push notification only after 3+ days of inactivity (not daily)
6.  Send training reminders during historically active times per user
7.  Allow managers to set department-wide "blackout windows" for training
8.  Provide month-end-specific ultra-short scenarios (2-min invoice fraud focus)
9.  Reduce session size dynamically during detected low-activity weeks
10. Offer optional weekend catch-up sessions with reduced question count

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P5: New employees are overwhelmed by multiple unfamiliar metrics
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  Add onboarding tooltip explaining each metric on first login
2.  Simplify My Score to show only composite risk + weakest category initially
3.  Add plain-language labels: "Risk Score = how vulnerable you are (lower = better)"
4.  Hide RF prediction and SHAP from employee view (admin-only data)
5.  Rename "Risk Score" to "Security Score" and invert (higher = better)
6.  Add a brief tutorial/walkthrough for first-time users
7.  Show "What this means for you" plain-language summary above technical details
8.  Use colour coding consistently (green = safe across all metrics)
9.  Display detailed metrics only after 3+ sessions (avoid overwhelming new users)
10. Add "What does this mean?" expandable helper text under each metric card


================================================================================
SECTION 4: BEST 2 ALTERNATIVES PER PROBLEM
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P1: Employees rush through training without reading content
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
-> (A) Add minimum engagement timer (15s) and require reading explanation
   before advancing. Simple frontend change, directly prevents speed-rushing.
-> (B) Tighten spaced repetition - shorten the re-appearance window for
   incorrectly answered scenarios (e.g., 12h instead of 24-72h). Existing
   adaptive engine supports this; requires parameter adjustment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P2: Admin cannot interpret or act on risk scores meaningfully
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Show SHAP-based per-category breakdown and weakest category on the
   admin user table. Gives immediate actionable visibility without extra clicks.
2. Add one-click "Assign remedial training" with auto-selected categories
   based on weakest areas. Turns insight into direct action.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P3: Training feedback exists but employees do not use it to improve
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Require reading the full explanation (minimum 5s display) before the
   "Next" button enables. Zero backend change, immediate engagement improvement.
2. Show per-category mastery breakdown on the main employee dashboard
   (currently only visible on My Score page). Makes weakness visible where
   Suman actually looks - the dashboard he opens every time.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P4: Training engagement drops to zero during peak work periods
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Allow managers to declare department-wide "busy windows" (e.g., month-end)
   where the system automatically offers 5-question micro-sessions instead of 20.
2. Offer 5-question micro-sessions as a permanent alternative. Maintains
   learning continuity without full 20-question commitment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P5: New employees are overwhelmed by multiple unfamiliar metrics
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Add first-login onboarding walkthrough with tooltip explanations for
   each dashboard element. Low effort, addresses confusion at the source.
2. Simplify default employee view: show only composite risk score and
   weakest category with a plain-language SHAP summary. Hide RF prediction
   details behind an expandable "Technical details" section.

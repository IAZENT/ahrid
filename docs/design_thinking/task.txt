Key Insight: Hidden Need & Pain Point
Hidden Need: Priya needs training that connects directly to her role as an accountant - she becomes genuinely engaged when scenarios involve invoice fraud or fake NIC Asia Bank payment confirmations, but disengages when content feels generic.
Pain Point: Despite AHRIP's adaptive engine, Priya still earns XP by rushing through scenarios - the gamification rewards speed of completion, not depth of understanding. She levels up without feeling more secure, revealing a gap between metric compliance and genuine behavioural change.
How Might We Question
How might we design AHRIP's training experience so that employees like Priya genuinely remember security concepts - with scenarios that feel directly relevant to their specific job role, questions that are interesting rather than boring, pacing that avoids overwhelm, and rewards that reflect actual skill growth rather than just click-through speed?
Reflection
Mapping all five dimensions - Sees, Says, Thinks, Does, Feels - reveals the crucial gap between surface behaviour and inner experience. Without this map, we might assume that leaderboards and badges fully motivate Priya. But empathy mapping shows her hidden thought: "I levelled up but don't feel safer." This moves design from assumption ("gamification works") to truth ("gamification without role-relevance creates hollow compliance"). The map also surfaces that Priya's most genuine engagement happens when LLM feedback explains why she was wrong - pointing to a design opportunity around feedback depth, not just reward frequency. Mapping dimensions transforms vague hunches into specific, actionable design directions.

2. Five Whys
1. Why do employees complete AHRIP training scenarios and earn XP, yet fail to retain cybersecurity knowledge and apply safe behaviours in their real daily work?
→ Because training feels like a checkbox task - employees at Himalayan Finance rush through scenarios to collect XP and complete the requirement, without deeply reading or thinking about the content.
2. Why do employees treat it as a checkbox?
→ Because most scenarios are not visibly connected to the employee's specific job role - an accountant sees generic phishing emails, not invoice fraud or fake NIC Asia Bank payment confirmations that feel personally relevant.
3. Why doesn't role relevance feel visible?
→ Because the adaptive engine selects scenarios based on past performance and Thompson Sampling, but does not communicate its reasoning to the learner - employees see a scenario card with no context about why it was chosen for them.
4. Why does the engine not communicate its reasoning?
→ Because there is no spaced repetition or post-training reinforcement loop - once a scenario is answered, it is archived. Knowledge is tested once and never revisited, so retention decays rapidly without review cycles.
5. Why is there no reinforcement loop?
→ Because AHRIP's primary success metric is the risk score (Random Forest composite) - the system is optimised to move a measurable number, not to build lasting behavioural habits. Gamification rewards completion volume, not comprehension depth.
Root Cause Identified
AHRIP optimises for measurable output (risk scores, XP, completion rates) rather than lasting behavioural change. The platform delivers technically compliant training but fails to close the loop between adaptive selection, learner understanding, role-relevance communication, and long-term knowledge retention. Without visible personalisation, post-session reinforcement, and comprehension-linked rewards, employees learn to complete training - not to think securely.
Design opportunity: Introduce transparent adaptive reasoning ("This scenario targets invoice fraud risks in your accounting role"), spaced-repetition review triggers, micro-reflection prompts after wrong answers, and comprehension-based XP multipliers - shifting AHRIP from a compliance engine to a genuine behavioural change platform.

Comments on User Experience
Employees are interrupted in the open-plan office, often losing scenario context due to timers advancing. Cognitive load is high during peak banking hours (9-11 AM, end of month). Admins want explainable risk scores, not just outputs. Leaderboard anonymisation is positively received. New hires are confused by the separation between XP level and risk score.
Friction Point
Fast incorrect answers reduce difficulty, unintentionally rewarding low effort. This creates a feedback loop of easier questions, inflated XP, and reduced learning value - leading to disengagement and false confidence.
Opportunity for Innovation
Make adaptation transparent by explaining scenario selection. Add reflection prompts after mistakes to reinforce learning. Optimise notification timing based on user activity patterns (avoiding peak reconciliation hours). Reward reasoning quality, not just correctness, to encourage deeper engagement.
General Thoughts & Design Direction
AHRIP has strong technical capabilities (ML, LLMs, real-time feeds, accessibility). The key gap is explainability for users. The system should surface adaptive logic, connect training to job context, and align XP with real skill development. Focus should shift from hidden personalisation to transparent behaviour-driven learning and security awareness improvement.

4. POV Statement
Priya, an accountant at Himalayan Finance Ltd in Kathmandu using AHRIP for mandatory security training, needs training scenarios that feel directly relevant to her finance role, reinforce knowledge over time, and reward comprehension not just completion - because she currently treats training as a checkbox task, clicking through scenarios to earn XP without internalising the content, leaving her genuinely vulnerable to role-specific threats like invoice fraud and fake NIC Asia Bank phishing emails despite being technically "compliant."

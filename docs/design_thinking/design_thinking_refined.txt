CORRECTED SYSTEM REALITY (BSc build - verified from source code)
----------------------------------------------------------------
What EMPLOYEE actually sees on opening the webapp:
- Dashboard: composite risk score number + colored badge, sessions completed count, weeks-with-activity trend
- Training: adaptive 20-question MCQ session, per-question timer, category badge, difficulty 1-3, progress bar. Explanation + mastery % shown after each answer. No XP, no levels, no badges, no streaks, no leaderboard.
- My Score: detailed risk breakdown by category, SHAP explanation, Random Forest prediction + confidence, behavioural archetype label, 8-week accuracy history
- History: past session results with accuracy
- Profile: personal details, job role, department
- Evaluation: self-assessment form
- Transparency: how the risk model works

What ADMIN actually sees:
- Admin Panel: total users, active scenarios, threats ingested (24h), attempts (24h), RF model status, background job controls (Ingest Feed / Retrain Models)
- Users: create, edit, deactivate, assign roles/job_roles
- Scenarios: CRUD for training items (MCQ, T/F, identify_threat)
- Threat Feeds: ingested phishing URLs, brand targets, conversion status
- Password Resets: approve/reject employee reset requests
- Evaluation Admin: view evaluation responses

What MANAGER actually sees:
- Team Overview: avg risk, high/critical count, sessions/week, trend direction + %
- Team: individual member profiles, job role, risk level, weakest category, sessions this week, last active. Can assign targeted training.
- Clusters: K-Means archetype distribution with labels + colours
- Reports: aggregated analytics, CSV export

Key backend facts (verified):
- SESSION_SIZE = 20. No gamification (XP/streaks/badges) in this build.
- Adaptive engine = mastery-weighted selection, NOT Thompson Sampling here.
- Has target_roles field on scenarios + applies_to_role() logic.
- Has ROLE_CATEGORY_PRIORITY mapping per job role.
- Has spaced repetition via RISK_LEVEL_TO_SPACING_HOURS.
- Notifications: only training_assigned and risk_escalation.
- Categories: phishing_email, smishing, vishing, physical_security, password_hygiene, usb_baiting, social_engineering, data_handling.

1. EMPATHY MAP
================================================================================

Persona: Suman Shrestha, 28, Accountant at Himalayan Finance Ltd, Putalisadak.
Uses Tally, Excel, NIC Asia Bank portal daily. Commutes from Lalitpur on microbus.
Monthly reconciliation crunch: 25th-5th. Works in open-plan office.

SEES:
- AHRIP dashboard showing a risk score number (e.g., 67) and a colored badge ("medium")
- "Sessions completed" card showing a low number because he only does mandatory sessions
- Training page with 20-question progress bar, timer counting seconds, category badge
- Scenario cards with generic titles like "Bank security alert email" - not NIC Asia specific
- "My Score" page with Random Forest prediction, SHAP bars, and a cluster label he doesn't understand
- Colleagues in the open-plan office clicking through training during lunch break

SAYS:
- "I finished my 20 questions for this week, done."
- "This says my risk score is 67 - what does that even mean for my actual job?"
- "Why am I getting a question about USB baiting? I only process invoices in Tally."
- "Can I do this later? Month-end reconciliation is due and my manager is asking for the VAT report."
- "The system says my weakest category is phishing_email, but it didn't tell me why this specific question was picked."

THINKS:
- "I answered all 20 questions but I don't actually feel safer at work."
- "Why is there a question about physical tailgating? I sit at a desk all day."
- "The timer is running while my phone rings - I'm not even reading the scenario."
- "My risk score dropped but I still almost clicked a suspicious link in Outlook last week."
- "I see 'mastery 45%' after a wrong answer, but I can just click Next and forget it."
- "The cluster label says 'casual_rusher' - that's embarrassing if my manager sees it."

DOES:
- Clicks "Start training" and speeds through 20 MCQs during his 30-minute lunch break
- Selects answers quickly without reading the full scenario content
- Skips reading the explanation card after wrong answers to save time
- Opens training during month-end, gets interrupted by Tally alerts, returns to find the timer advanced
- Checks "My Score" once after first session, sees "unknown" risk level, never returns
- Ignores the SHAP explanation panel because it feels like math homework

FEELS:
- Frustrated that 20 questions interrupt actual accounting deadlines
- Anxious about the visible risk score - what if his manager sees it in Team view?
- Bored by generic scenarios that don't relate to invoice fraud or fake payment confirmations
- Mildly curious when the mastery % changes, but no lasting motivation
- Confused by the difference between composite risk score, RF prediction, and cluster archetype
- Relieved when the 20-question session finally ends

KEY INSIGHT:
Suman completes the mandatory 20-question sessions but does not engage with the learning content.
The gap between "completing training" and "understanding security" is where real vulnerability lives.
The system tracks his responses and updates a risk score, but there is no mechanism that forces
him to actually read and retain explanations. The timer, lack of role-specific context, and
absence of comprehension-linked rewards all enable speed-rushing.

================================================================================
2. FIVE WHYS
================================================================================

1. Why do employees at Himalayan Finance complete AHRIP training sessions, yet fail to retain cybersecurity knowledge in daily work?
   -> Because training feels like a checkbox task - Suman rushes through 20 MCQs during lunch to satisfy the requirement, without deeply reading scenarios or explanations.

2. Why does Suman treat it as a checkbox?
   -> Because scenarios are not visibly connected to his accounting role - he sees generic "bank security alert" emails, not fake NIC Asia Bank payment confirmations or eSewa invoice fraud that matches his actual daily work.

3. Why doesn't role relevance feel visible to Suman?
   -> Because although the backend has target_roles and ROLE_CATEGORY_PRIORITY, the frontend scenario card shows no label explaining why this question was chosen for him. He sees a random card with a category badge and difficulty number, but zero context linking it to his job.

4. Why is there no post-session reinforcement?
   -> Because once a scenario is answered, it enters the spaced-repetition window but the employee never sees it again until the system silently allows it. There is no active review prompt, no micro-quiz, no reminder of past mistakes. Knowledge is tested once and decays.

5. Why does AHRIP not close the reinforcement loop?
   -> Because the system's primary success metric is the Random Forest composite risk score - it is optimised to move a measurable number, not to build lasting behavioural habits. More sessions = more data = better model. The system rewards completion volume (attempts feed the risk model), not comprehension depth.

ROOT CAUSE:
AHRIP optimises for measurable outputs (risk scores, completion counts, model accuracy) rather
than lasting behavioural change. Without visible personalisation on the UI, post-session
reinforcement, comprehension-linked session design, and role-context communication, employees
learn to complete training - not to think securely.

================================================================================
PROBLEM 1: Employees rush through training without reading content
================================================================================
User perspective: Suman sees 20 MCQ scenarios, selects answers fast to finish
quickly. The timer shows seconds ticking. He gets a mastery % update regardless
of whether he read the explanation. The system records an attempt and feeds the
risk model - completion is counted, understanding is not.

10 FEASIBLE SOLUTIONS:
1. Add minimum read-time per scenario (e.g., 10s before submit button enables)
2. Show a one-sentence comprehension check after the explanation card
3. Weight mastery update by time-spent-reading, not just correctness
4. Introduce "reflection prompt" after wrong answers requiring typed one-sentence response
5. Reduce session length from 20 to 10 questions but add depth per question
6. Display scenario relevance tag: "Why this matters for your accounting role: ..."
7. Require viewing the full explanation for 5 seconds before advancing to next question
8. Add session quality rating: flag sessions completed under 3 minutes for manager review
9. Show "learning quality score" alongside accuracy to differentiate engagement
10. Surface previously-missed scenarios first in next session with "Retry this concept" label

BEST 2 SOLUTIONS:
-> (A) Add minimum engagement timer (10s) + require explanation viewing before advancing.
   Simple frontend change in TrainingPage.tsx, directly prevents speed-rushing.
-> (B) Surface previously-missed scenarios with "Retry: you missed this on [date]" label.
   Uses existing spaced-repetition backend logic; adds visible reinforcement loop.

================================================================================
PROBLEM 2: Scenarios feel generic and disconnected from employee's actual role
================================================================================
User perspective: Suman is an accountant but gets questions about USB baiting
and physical tailgating. The backend has target_roles and ROLE_CATEGORY_PRIORITY,
but he never sees WHY a scenario was picked. He doesn't see why a social engineering
question about badge access matters to someone who processes invoices in Tally all day.

10 FEASIBLE SOLUTIONS:
1. Surface "target_roles" info in the scenario card UI as a relevance tag
2. Prioritize role-matched scenarios higher in the adaptive selection (already partially done; expose it)
3. Add Kathmandu-specific scenario packs (fake NIC Asia payment alerts, eSewa refund SMS, fake NRB audit letters)
4. Show "Selected for you: invoice fraud risk in your accounting role" label on each card
5. Allow employees to rate scenario relevance after each session (1-5 stars, feedback loop)
6. Let admin create department-specific scenario collections
7. Add a "relevance match %" metric visible to managers showing role-match rate per employee
8. Generate contextualised scenario introductions via LLM at serve-time: "As an accountant, you often receive..."
9. Allow employees to choose a focus area before starting a session (e.g., "Invoice fraud this week")
10. Create visual_html templates mimicking actual Nepali financial interfaces (NIC Asia login, eSewa wallet)

BEST 2 SOLUTIONS:
-> (A) Show transparent adaptive reasoning on each scenario card: one label line.
   Uses existing target_roles data; minimal frontend change, maximum perceived relevance.
-> (B) Create Kathmandu-localised, role-specific scenario packs with real Nepali brand visuals.
   High impact on engagement; seed_scenarios.py can be extended with Nepal-specific content.

================================================================================
Admin perspective: Rajesh sees a user's composite risk score of 67 on the manager

to take. The admin Users page shows email, role, job, active - but no risk score,

10 FEASIBLE SOLUTIONS:
1. Add per-category risk breakdown visible on admin user detail page
2. Show SHAP feature importance summary for each high-risk user on manager team page
3. Generate automated coaching recommendation per high-risk user: "Assign: phishing_email, data_handling"
4. Add "weakest category" and "risk score" columns to the admin users table
5. Create one-click "assign remedial training" button on manager team page using existing assign-training API
6. Show historical risk trend (last 4 weeks) for each user on manager hover/expand
7. Add risk_escalation notification to admin inbox, not just employee
8. Display cluster archetype description + intervention text on manager team page (already partially there; expand)
9. Export risk report per department as CSV for management review (reports API exists; add filtering)
10. Add natural-language summary: "User X struggles with phishing_email - 3 wrong answers in last 2 sessions"

BEST 2 SOLUTIONS:
-> (A) Add weakest_category + risk_score columns to admin Users page + manager Team page.
   Uses existing backend data; turns raw roster into actionable intelligence.
-> (B) Add one-click "Assign remedial training" button on manager Team page with auto-selected
   categories based on weakest areas. Uses existing /manager/team/<id>/assign-training endpoint;
   turns insight into direct action.

PROBLEM 4: New employees are confused by multiple metrics (risk score, RF prediction, cluster)
================================================================================
User perspective: A new hire at Himalayan Finance sees three labels on their first
day: Dashboard says "Current risk: - (unknown)", My Score says "Not enough attempts
yet to compute a risk score", and after 3 sessions they see "composite_score: 72",
"RF predicted_risk_level: medium", and "archetype_label: casual_rusher". They don't
understand the relationship. "Am I doing well or badly?"

10 FEASIBLE SOLUTIONS:
1. Add first-login onboarding walkthrough with tooltip explanations for each dashboard element
2. Combine into a single "Security Readiness" score shown prominently (invert risk so higher = better)
3. Add contextual label on dashboard: "Risk score = vulnerability detected from your answers"
4. Show a visual relationship diagram between composite score, RF prediction, and cluster on My Score
5. Rename "Risk Score" to "Security Awareness Score" and invert (higher = better) to align intuition
6. Add a brief tutorial/walkthrough for first-time users before first session
7. Show "Your accuracy is improving but risk is still elevated because..." dynamic explanation
8. Use color coding consistently (green = safe/positive for ALL metrics)
9. Display simplified risk score only after 3+ sessions; show "Building your profile..." before
10. Add "What does this mean?" expandable helper text under each metric on My Score

BEST 2 SOLUTIONS:
-> (A) Add first-login onboarding walkthrough with tooltip explanations for dashboard elements.
   Low effort, addresses confusion at the source before bad mental models form.
-> (B) Rename and reframe: show "Security Awareness Score" (inverted risk, 0-100, higher = better)
   as the PRIMARY metric on the dashboard. Aligns mental model with "improving" direction.

================================================================================
PROBLEM 5: Training engagement drops to zero during peak work periods
================================================================================
User perspective: During month-end reconciliation (25th-5th), Suman has zero
bandwidth for a 20-question session. Tally is open, invoices are piling up, VAT
reports are due, and the manager is asking for numbers. He skips training for
3-5 days, then faces a 20-question backlog that feels overwhelming. There is no
micro-session option, no streak to maintain, and no pause mechanism.

10 FEASIBLE SOLUTIONS:
1. Offer 5-question "micro sessions" as an alternative to 20-question standard sessions
2. Allow employees to set "busy hours" where notifications are suppressed
3. Auto-detect low-engagement periods and reduce session recommendation frequency
4. Schedule training reminders based on individual activity patterns (avoid 9-11 AM, month-end)
5. Introduce "session pause" - save progress and resume later without losing place
6. Allow managers to set department-wide "blackout windows" for training (e.g., month-end)
7. Provide month-end-specific ultra-short scenarios (2-min invoice fraud focus)
8. Reduce session size to 10 questions during detected peak periods (configurable per department)
9. Offer optional weekend catch-up sessions with "focus mode" (only weakest category)
10. Add a "Save for later" bookmark on scenarios so employees can preview and return

BEST 2 SOLUTIONS:
-> (A) Offer 5-question micro-sessions as a permanent alternative alongside 20-question standard.
   Minimal backend change (parameterise SESSION_SIZE); maintains learning continuity during busy periods.
-> (B) Add session pause/resume so employees can answer 5 questions, pause, and finish later.
   Uses existing session_id tracking; removes the "all-or-nothing" barrier that kills engagement.

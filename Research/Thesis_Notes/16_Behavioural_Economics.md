# Behavioural Economics in Cybersecurity

> Maps to **Objective 1** - Human cognitive vulnerabilities

---

## Key Concepts

### Cognitive Biases Exploited by Attackers
- **Authority bias** - Phishing emails from "CEO" or "IT department"
- **Urgency/scarcity** - "Your account will be locked in 24 hours"
- **Curiosity** - "Click here to see who viewed your profile"
- **Reciprocity** - Free USB drives with malware (USB baiting)
- **Social proof** - "Your colleagues have already completed this"

### Nudge Theory (Thaler & Sunstein, 2008)
- Choice architecture can guide better security decisions
- AHRID's nudges: adaptive difficulty, SHAP risk explanations, gamification

### Prospect Theory (Kahneman & Tversky)
- People are risk-averse for gains but risk-seeking for losses
- Security framing: "You could lose access" vs "Keep your account safe"

### Security Fatigue
- Too many warnings → users ignore them all
- Stanton et al. - password fatigue, alert fatigue
- AHRID addresses this with personalized, adaptive training (not repetitive)

## Relevance to AHRID
- Scenario design exploits known cognitive biases (realistic social engineering)
- Risk scores help users understand their own vulnerability patterns
- SHAP explanations counter the "it won't happen to me" bias
